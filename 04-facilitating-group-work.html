Three steps, every time

  • Introduce the task. Small and concrete beats big and open.
  • Give a stated, fixed amount of time. Say it out loud.
  • Require something back. Groups that know they report out behave differently.
Think-Pair-Share is the minimum viable version and needs no preparation at all.

Silence is a design problem

If nobody is talking, the question was too big, too vague, or too risky to get wrong in public.

Making it smaller fixes it more often than encouragement does.

"Discuss the tradeoffs" gets silence. "Which of these two would you ship, and why" gets an argument.

Roles spread what the task does not

Assign

  • Coordinator
  • Recorder
  • Skeptic
  • Presenter

And rotate

  • Otherwise the same confident student carries every group
  • Which is bad for them and worse for everyone else

Positive interdependence

Each person has to believe their piece matters to the outcome.

If one capable person could do the task alone, one capable person will do it alone, whatever you do with the chairs.

That is also the test for your own RLO.

The move

Design participation in,
rather than asking for it

You do not get equal participation by requesting it.

You get it by building a task the group cannot finish without it.
