Teaching Statements
and Portfolios

Guest: Johanna Yunker, Graduate School Office of Professional Development

No Wednesday this week

Veterans' Day. This is a single Friday session.

It is also the session that looks furthest ahead. The statement is a document you need on the market, and the evidence it requires is being generated this semester.

The problem, three years from now

You sit down to write two pages and you need specifics. A class that went well. A change you made after feedback. Evidence something worked.

You will remember none of it, because you wrote none of it down.

The other failure

Most first drafts are a narrative of how the writer came to love teaching.

Search committees read hundreds of those.

Do not tell the story of how you got into teaching. Start with a specific class.

Value, example, evidence

Tell

  • "I stress creative thinking in all my classes."
  • A claim anyone can make

Show

  • The value, then what you actually do, then what happened
  • Each step harder to write and more credible
YOUR TURN · 8 MIN

Write one paragraph

Pick one thing you actually believe about teaching. Name it, give a concrete example from this semester, then say what happened as a result.

Three sentences. The third is the hard one.

Collect as you go

  • Syllabi
  • Anecdotes, written down while you remember them
  • End of semester evaluations
  • Your peer review reports
  • Mid-semester feedback results
Easy to save now. Impossible to reconstruct later. A folder is enough. It does not need organizing yet.

You already have three of these

  • Your Reflection 4 video analysis
  • Your peer review learning report, due Nov 20
  • Whatever mid-semester feedback you collected

That is more portfolio evidence than most people have when they start looking.

What if you leave academia?

Communication, supervision, organization, handling difficult conversations.

Exactly what industry interviews probe, and the specific example you would put in a teaching statement is the specific example you would give there.

Same move, different framing. "When I present to non-expert audiences I..." beats "I have strong presentation skills."

The move

Keep the evidence
while it still exists

Everything else here is writing advice you could get in three years.

The evidence has a shelf life, and this semester is generating it.

Forty statements on the pile. Yours names a specific thing you changed and what happened when you did.

And if neither is yours yet: what do you want out of the job you actually have?

Before next time

Make a folder. Put in it your video analysis, your feedback, and one paragraph about something specific that happened in your section.
