Assessment and grading

Two graders, one rubric, an eight point gap

Read this before the session. You are not expected to have had this happen to you. You are expected to have thought about it for five minutes before anyone asks you what you would do.

You are one of two TAs grading homework 4 for CS 356, Algorithms, taught by Dr. Castellanos. The class has a hundred and eighty students and you split them down the middle.

What happens

You are about ninety papers in when a student posts on the course forum that their friend got eight points more for what they say is the same answer. Within an hour the thread has thirty replies and somebody has made a spreadsheet.

You check. Your average on the recurrence question is 78. The other TA's is 86. You are using the same rubric, which says, in full: Correctness of the recurrence: 6 points.

You have been giving two out of six for a correct recurrence with no working shown, on the grounds that the question said to justify it. The other TA has been giving five, on the grounds that the answer is right. Neither of you knew the other was doing it. You have both already returned about forty papers.

Dr. Castellanos is at a conference and has said they are reachable for emergencies, which you are fairly sure this is not, or possibly is.

What you know

  • A hundred and eighty students. Roughly eighty papers already returned between you.
  • The full text of that rubric line is the six words quoted above. There is no breakdown.
  • Regrade requests are open for one week after return and go through the course tool.
  • The forum thread is visible to the whole class, including the spreadsheet.
  • Dr. Castellanos returns Monday. Grades are not due until the following Friday.

What you do not know

  • How many of the returned papers are affected.
  • Whether the other TA changed their approach partway through their own half.
  • Whether Dr. Castellanos has a view about what that line was supposed to mean.

The three questions

  1. What would you do in the first five minutes?
    Not the ideal response. The one you would actually give, under time pressure.
  2. What does your course actually let you do?
    Everyone answers this one, about their own course. Your policy, or what your instructor has told you.
  3. Where did your group disagree?
    Do not smooth this over. People assisting different courses should not agree, and the disagreement is the most useful thing you can report.

What groups have said

Nothing published yet. Summaries of what groups decided are added at the end of each semester, with names and courses removed.
