Building an inclusive community

The group that split

Read this before the session. You are not expected to have had this happen to you. You are expected to have thought about it for five minutes before anyone asks you what you would do.

You advise five project teams in CS 338, Software Engineering Studio, taught by Professor Moreau. Teams of four, one project, the whole semester.

What happens

One of your teams has been quietly going wrong for a month. Two members do most of the talking and, from what you can see, most of the work. A third says almost nothing in check-ins. The fourth has not pushed a commit in three weeks.

At this week's check-in the two vocal members talk over the third twice. You notice it and do not say anything, which you are still thinking about.

That evening one of them messages you asking how to "remove" the other two from the team before the final submission, because they are "carrying dead weight".

Twenty minutes later the quiet third member emails you, separately, to say they have opened four pull requests since September and all four were closed without review, and they did not know who to tell.

What you know

  • The team project is thirty per cent of the course grade. A peer evaluation at the end is worth a further ten.
  • Professor Moreau's stated policy is that teams do not change after week four. It is week nine.
  • You can see the repository, including who opened what and who closed it.
  • Neither message was sent to the whole team. Each person thinks they are telling you something in confidence.
  • Final submission is in four weeks.

What you do not know

  • Why those pull requests were closed.
  • Whether the fourth member, the one who has gone quiet, is in any difficulty.
  • Whether anyone has said any of this to anyone else on the team.

The three questions

  1. What would you do in the first five minutes?
    Not the ideal response. The one you would actually give, under time pressure.
  2. What does your course actually let you do?
    Everyone answers this one, about their own course. Your policy, or what your instructor has told you.
  3. Where did your group disagree?
    Do not smooth this over. People assisting different courses should not agree, and the disagreement is the most useful thing you can report.

What groups have said

Nothing published yet. Summaries of what groups decided are added at the end of each semester, with names and courses removed.

Every course, instructor and student in this scenario is invented. It does not describe a real person or a real incident.  All scenarios
