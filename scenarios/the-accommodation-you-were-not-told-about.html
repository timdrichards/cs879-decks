Equity and accessibility

The accommodation you were not told about

Read this before the session. You are not expected to have had this happen to you. You are expected to have thought about it for five minutes before anyone asks you what you would do.

You run a lab section for CS 118, Programming Foundations, taught by Professor Alvarez. Today's lab ends with a twenty minute quiz on the whiteboard.

What happens

You announce the quiz. A student, Dana, comes up while everyone else is settling and says quietly that they get time and a half, that they normally take quizzes at the testing centre, and that nobody told them there would be a quiz in lab today.

You have not been sent a letter. You have no list. You have never been told anything about accommodations in this course by anyone.

It is 3:40. Your lab ends at 4:05 and another section comes into the room at 4:15. Professor Alvarez is teaching a lecture until 4:00 and does not read email while teaching. Twenty-nine other students are waiting for you to hand out the quiz.

Dana is standing there waiting for an answer and the room has gone quiet enough that the front two rows can hear.

What you know

  • Thirty students in the lab. The quiz is worth ten per cent of the lab grade.
  • You have never been given a roster of accommodations for this course.
  • The room is booked by another section from 4:15.
  • Disability Services closes at 5:00 today.
  • You do not have the authority to change what an assessment is worth or when it happens.

What you do not know

  • Whether the accommodation exists on file.
  • Whether Professor Alvarez knows about it and did not pass it on.
  • Whether a twenty minute in-lab quiz is even the kind of assessment the accommodation covers.

The three questions

  1. What would you do in the first five minutes?
    Not the ideal response. The one you would actually give, under time pressure.
  2. What does your course actually let you do?
    Everyone answers this one, about their own course. Your policy, or what your instructor has told you.
  3. Where did your group disagree?
    Do not smooth this over. People assisting different courses should not agree, and the disagreement is the most useful thing you can report.

What groups have said

Nothing published yet. Summaries of what groups decided are added at the end of each semester, with names and courses removed.

Every course, instructor and student in this scenario is invented. It does not describe a real person or a real incident.  All scenarios
