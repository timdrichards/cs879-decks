Being a responsible employee

The office hour that changed

Read this before the session. You are not expected to have had this happen to you. You are expected to have thought about it for five minutes before anyone asks you what you would do.

You hold office hours for CS 262, Discrete Structures, taught by Dr. Bhatt, in a shared room with two other TAs at the other end of the table.

What happens

A student, Theo, comes in at ten to five about a deadline they missed. You start talking about the extension policy. They stop you and say the reason they missed it, and it is not about the course.

What they describe is a situation at home involving a partner. They mention, in passing and without being asked, that they have been staying with a friend because they are afraid to go back to their apartment.

Then they say: please do not tell anyone, I only need the extension.

The other two TAs are eight feet away. It is 4:50 on a Friday. You have no authority to grant an extension. You are a responsible employee of the university, and you know what that means, and you now have about four seconds before your silence becomes its own answer.

What you know

  • You are a responsible employee and are required to report disclosures of this kind to the university.
  • You cannot grant an extension. Only Dr. Bhatt can.
  • You have the Dean of Students page and the confidential counselling line saved, because you were told to in week one.
  • The room is shared and is not private.
  • Dr. Bhatt is contactable by email but it is Friday evening.

What you do not know

  • Anything about the situation beyond the two sentences you have been told.
  • Whether the student has told anyone else, or wants to.
  • What the student expects you to do with what they have just said.

The three questions

  1. What would you do in the first five minutes?
    Not the ideal response. The one you would actually give, under time pressure.
  2. What does your course actually let you do?
    Everyone answers this one, about their own course. Your policy, or what your instructor has told you.
  3. Where did your group disagree?
    Do not smooth this over. People assisting different courses should not agree, and the disagreement is the most useful thing you can report.

What groups have said

Nothing published yet. Summaries of what groups decided are added at the end of each semester, with names and courses removed.

Every course, instructor and student in this scenario is invented. It does not describe a real person or a real incident.  All scenarios
