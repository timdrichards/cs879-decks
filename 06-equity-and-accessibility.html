Act two: designing for learning

Where we are

Unit 5 was designing an activity. Today asks who that activity actually works for.

Two different things sit here. Fairness in how you spend attention, which is about your habits. And accommodation, which is law with procedures.

Equality is not equity

Equality is the same treatment. Equity is the treatment that produces a fair outcome.

Both are defensible in a queue. Drifting between them depending on who is standing there is not.

IN PAIRS · 6 MIN

Office hours, ninety minutes, six people

Student A learned this before class started and wants to go further. Student B has not understood the last three weeks and needs far more than their share.

How do you split the time, and what do you say to each?

Should you TA off the clock?

One student can reach you outside office hours because they live in your building. Another cannot.

That is an advantage with nothing to do with their work.

"Sorry, because I'm a TA I can only help during office hours." That is the whole script, and it is the fair rule even though it feels cold.
BE HONEST · 4 MIN

How do you pick who to help next?

  • In order of arrival
  • Whoever is physically nearest
  • Whoever seems most upset
  • Whoever you find easiest to talk to

That last one is where bias lives, and it is invisible from the inside.

They read the room differently than you do

For some students

  • A raised hand is a normal request
  • Asking early is efficient

For others

  • It is a public admission of inadequacy
  • Better to struggle quietly
So the same office hours produce very different value, and the students who need it most are often least likely to ask.

Accommodation letters

  • The letter goes to your instructor, not to you
  • Your job is to implement what the determination says
  • When it is unclear, ask the instructor. Do not improvise.
  • Never negotiate an accommodation directly with a student
And never apply a late penalty to someone with a pending request without checking first.

Universal design

Designing so fewer people need an individual accommodation in the first place.

  • Captions on anything with audio
  • Real headings, not bold text
  • Contrast that survives a projector
  • Do not put meaning in color alone
Same standard applies to your RLO. If it fails with a screen reader it is not reusable, which was criterion one.
IN GROUPS · 8 MIN

What would you actually do?

Take one: low vision, blindness, hearing impairment, or a documented accommodation for extra time.

What do you change about your section, concretely, this week?

Conflicts of interest

Friends, partners, people you want to impress, people you dislike, members of an organization you want to join.

Tell your supervisor and abide by what they decide.

Declaring it is the whole obligation. Managing it quietly is the thing that goes wrong.

The move

Make the rule
before the case arrives

Decide how you allocate office hours. Decide you help only on the clock. Decide accommodations route through the instructor.

Then you are applying a policy rather than judging a person while they watch.

Before next time

For one office hours session, write down the order you helped people in and why you picked each person next.

Do not try to change anything. Just record it.

The pattern is the finding, and most people are surprised by theirs.
