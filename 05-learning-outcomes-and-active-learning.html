Learning Outcomes
and Active Learning

Act two: designing for learning

Where we are

Unit 4 was running a group task. Today is saying what the task is for, precisely enough that you could tell whether it worked.

Your practicum asks what you expect your change to do. That is an intended learning outcome, and you write one today.

The problem with "understand"

"Help them understand recursion" sounds like an outcome and is not one.

How would you know whether it happened?

If you cannot say what the session was for, you cannot tell afterward whether it worked, so you end up judging your teaching by how it felt.

Start with a verb someone can watch

Not these

  • Know
  • Understand
  • Appreciate
  • Be familiar with

These

  • Implement
  • Trace
  • Compare
  • Predict
  • Debug
  • Explain
The test: could you design a task that shows whether the student can do it?

Three kinds of outcome

  • Knowledge. What they can state.
  • Skills. What they can do.
  • Dispositions. How they approach a problem when nobody is watching.

CS teaching is heavy on the first two and mostly silent about the third.

Dispositions are what employers describe when asked what they want: algorithmic problem solver, collaborative, effective communicator.

Bloom's, as a ladder

  • Remember · Understand · Apply
  • Analyze · Evaluate · Create

A section living entirely at remember and understand is doing less than it could.

A section that jumps to create without the lower rungs leaves students stranded.

YOUR TURN · 5 MIN

Write one

Write an intended learning outcome for your own section or lab. Start with the verb. Name whether it is knowledge, a skill, or a disposition.

Then read it to your group and have them tell you whether it is observable.

Active learning

Anything students do in a classroom besides passively listening.

Techniques

  • Think-Pair-Share
  • Pair programming
  • Jigsaw

  • POGIL
  • Case studies
  • Collaborative coding

The technique matters less than whether students are producing something you can see.

IN PAIRS · 6 MIN

Match the technique

Given a scenario, which technique fits, and why not the others?

  • A concept that everyone thinks they understand and most do not
  • A skill that needs practice with immediate correction
  • A topic where students hold different and defensible positions

The move

State the outcome
before designing the activity

It feels backward because the activity is the fun part.

Same discipline as Unit 3. There you decided the criteria before applying them. Here you decide the outcome before building toward it.

Where this shows up later

If you stay in academia

  • Proposals and accreditation ask what they can do
  • Write "understand", get sent back

If you go to industry

  • This is writing acceptance criteria
  • "Finds last month's spend in thirty seconds"
What is Friday's section for? "Going over the homework" is not an answer, and you can hear it as you say it.

And if neither is yours: what do you want from the job you have?

Before next time

Write the outcome for your next discussion section before you plan it. One sentence, starting with a verb.

Afterward, ask whether you could tell if it happened.

If you could not, the outcome was not observable. Fix that rather than the activity.
