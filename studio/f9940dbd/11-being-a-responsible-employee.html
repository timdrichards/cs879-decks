Being a
Responsible Employee

Act three: reflecting on your own practice

Say early that this session may land on someone's own experience, that stepping out is fine, and that CCPH is available to them too.

This one has legal weight

These obligations started the day your appointment did, not today.

That gap is uncomfortable and worth naming. You got the short version in orientation for this reason.

If a student has already disclosed something to you this term, today tells you whether you handled it right. Come anyway if you think you did not.

The moment

A student stays after your section.

"Can I tell you something, but you can't tell anyone?"

The answer has to be no, and you have to say so before they continue.

The sentence

"I want to hear this. I should tell you I'm required to pass certain things on, so you can decide what you want to tell me."

It feels like it will end the conversation. It usually does not.

The alternative is a student who finds out afterward that you reported something they told you in confidence.

Which are you?

Responsible employee

  • Must report known or possible incidents
  • TAs generally fall here

Confidential employee

  • CWC, CCPH, Faculty and Staff Assistance Program
  • Does not trigger a report
Find out for certain rather than guessing. The difference determines what you can honestly promise.

What Title IX covers

  • Sex and gender-based discrimination and harassment
  • Sexual assault
  • Dating and domestic violence
  • Stalking
  • Retaliation

It also protects pregnant and parenting students, which comes up more than people expect.

That one is frequently mishandled as a scheduling problem rather than a rights question.

Scenarios

Adapted from real experiences of women graduate students in CICS.

  • Who has power here, and who is complicit?
  • Do you interrupt in the moment or afterward?
  • What exactly do you report, and to whom?
These are real. Say so. Keep the report-out on process rather than asking anyone to relive detail.

Where you send people

  • Dean of Students Office
  • Title IX Coordinator, Equal Opportunity and Access Office, 225 Bartlett
  • Center for Women and Community, confidential
  • CCPH, confidential
Telling a student which of these is confidential is genuinely useful and most people do not know.

Look after yourself

Hearing a disclosure is not neutral, and it can land on your own history without warning.

CCPH is available to you, not only to your students.

The move

Say what you are
before the conversation

Every hard part of this gets easier if the student knows the terms before deciding what to say.

Same principle as Unit 3. State the rule in advance and apply it, rather than deciding in the moment while someone watches.

Where this shows up later

If you stay in academia

  • You are a mandated reporter
  • Nobody is going to remind you again

If you go to industry

  • Every employer has a version of this
  • The ones people get wrong end careers
"Can I tell you something confidentially?" You have about two seconds, and the honest answer is usually no.

And if neither is yours: what do you want from the job you have?

Do not rush this slide. The question at the bottom is the real one and it is worth thirty seconds of silence. Say the line: if you cannot find what makes the job matter to you, come and tell me and we will work it out together. Some of them will.

Before next time

Find out for certain whether you are a responsible employee. Ask your instructor or the EOAO.

Then write down the sentence you would use. Have it ready.

You will not compose it well under pressure.
