What does your course actually say?

You were asked to find the AI policy for the course you are TAing. Round the room, one word each.

Permissive  ·  Restrictive  ·  Silent  ·  could not find it

If it says nothing, that is a finding, not a failure to find.

This was reading they did on their own time, as a TA duty rather than 879 homework, so do not spend the session on it. One word each, moving fast. Count the silents out loud. In past terms it has been about a third of the room, and that number lands harder than anything on a slide. If someone did not look, have them say so rather than guess, and give them ninety seconds to check now while the others go. Do not make it a thing.

In threes: draft two emails

A student in your section emails: "Am I allowed to use ChatGPT for homework 3? I don't want to get in trouble." You answer within the hour.
  • Two minutes. Names, your course, one surprise.
  • Email one. The reply you would really send.
  • Email two. Ask your instructor: did I get this right?
Threes, not pairs. Three gives you someone who disagrees. Say the line that no longer fits on the slide: whatever the syllabus says, their reply becomes the policy that student experiences. The first phase is the introductions proper. Two minutes, and it is worth the time because these are the people they will keep working with. Start the timer and let it run; if you leave it open it becomes five minutes. The second email is the harder one and the one that matters. A weak version asks 'what is our AI policy?' and gets no reply for a week. A good one names homework 3, offers the provisional answer they already sent, and asks to be corrected. Thirty seconds to read, ten to answer. Circulate. If a group is polishing email one and has not started two, push them on. The timer has three phases for exactly this reason: hit the chip when it is time.

Two groups read their second email aloud, not the first. Pick ones that are specific. Then the instruction that matters: send it now, in the room, from a laptop. A draft they carry out of here does not get sent. Give them a minute of silence to press send and say you will wait. This slide is the shock absorber for the whole session. Exit ticket

Write the exact sentence you are going to send your supervising instructor. Not a description of the email. The sentence itself, in the words you will actually use.

Copy the prompt into question two. Closes at 10:00, five minutes after we finish.

Do not post this prompt anywhere. It is the only part of the ticket that cannot be answered from a dorm room, which is what makes the ticket an attendance record rather than a form. Read it aloud once, start the timer, leave the slide up. The ticket closes at 10:00 whether or not everyone has finished, and that is fine at two points. Most of them will have sent the email already, in which case the sentence is a copy and paste and takes thirty seconds. That is the intended outcome.
