The discussion that will not start

A TA runs the same discussion plan twice. At 8:00 it dies. At 2:00 an argument breaks out and runs over. Same worksheet, same questions, same person.

Two minutes. Put the page up on the screen so the room is looking at the same thing. Read the situation aloud, or have someone read it. Then take factual questions only: how big is the section, had they done the reading, what time is it. Not "have they tried", which is advice with a question mark on the end and comes later. The scenario is public and they were asked to read it beforehand. Some will not have. Two minutes of reading is enough to take part, so do not make it a thing. The detail people miss on a first read is that the two sections are the same size on paper, twenty four each, but only fourteen turn up at 8:00. Point at it if nobody does.

In your pod: three questions

  • First five minutes. What would you actually do, not the ideal answer.
  • Your own course. Everyone answers. What does yours let you do?
  • Where did you disagree? Do not smooth it over. This is the one we want.

Pick a Chair to run it and report back, and a Scribe to write it down. Rotate every week.

Sixty seconds to pick roles, then seven minutes on the questions. Do not let the role-picking eat the discussion. Chair runs it and reports to the room. Scribe writes and posts to Canvas. Both rotate weekly, so by December everyone has done both. If a pod cannot decide, pick for them. Question two is the one that keeps this a practicum rather than a case study, so make sure every person answers it about their own course, including the quiet ones. That is the Chair's job and you should say so. Question three is the one that earns the format. A pod that reports agreement has usually not pushed. Circulate and ask directly: what did you disagree about? Last week the tendency was to pick the easiest version of a question. Watch for the same thing here: the comfortable answer is "cold call them", and the interesting one is why the same plan worked at 2:00.

Report out

Chairs only. About ninety seconds each, same three questions in order.

No volunteering. Every pod reports.

This is the part that replaces asking for volunteers, which last time produced exactly one. Every Chair reports, in pod order, and nobody has to put their hand up. Ninety seconds each. Cut them off gently and move on: five pods at ninety seconds is seven and a half minutes and you have eight. Make each Chair answer all three parts. A pod that describes a nice plan and cannot say what their course policy allows, or where they disagreed, has not done the exercise. Ask for the missing part rather than letting it go. Do not open it to the room between pods or you will not get through five. The room gets its turn on the next slide. If you are short of time, three pods instead of five, and say which pods will go first next week so the same people do not always miss out.

Pulling it together

Having heard all of those: would anyone have answered differently under their own course's policy?
Two minutes, once, now that every pod is on the table. This is the question that does not work if you ask it after each report, which is why it waits. Hands, then take two or three. What you are listening for is the contrast: the same situation handled differently because the courses are run differently. Say that out loud when you hear it, because it is the thing this course exists to show them and it is invisible from inside a single course. Last week two groups landed on opposite answers, one permissive and one restrictive, from a sample of two. Expect more of that with five. Then say the instruction that matters: anything the policy did not answer goes to their next staff meeting.

Practicum 1: Focus Statement

About 300 words. What your appointment actually involves, the one or two pieces you are choosing, and what bothers you about how it goes now.
  • The third part matters most. A focus that already annoys you will hold for fourteen weeks.
  • Scribes: post your pod's notes to Canvas first. Two minutes, then write.
Seventeen minutes of quiet. This is graded, ten points, and it is the spine of their whole practicum, so protect this block over everything else on this slide deck. Scribes post first, from the Studio notes discussion in the Week 2 module, using the four headings. Two minutes, then they write like everyone else. Circulate and read over shoulders. The instructor reads these during the session and tells people on the spot if the focus is too big, too small, or not really theirs, so do the same: you can spot the two failure modes quickly. Too big is "improve how students learn recursion". Too small is "reword one line of a rubric". Not theirs is anything they would not be doing if this course did not exist. The most common problem is inventing something rather than naming something that already annoys them. Ask: what part of your job did you complain about this week? That is usually the answer. They submit in Canvas before they leave. It stays open a week, but a draft they carry out does not get finished.

What did you pick?

One sentence each, round the room. The thing you are going to change, and why that one.

No explaining, no justifying. One sentence.

Twenty eight people in six minutes is about twelve seconds each, so this only works if you hold them to one sentence. Say that, and mean it on the first person, or it will not hold. The point is not the content, it is that hearing twenty seven other focuses tells them whether theirs is the right size. Several will quietly rewrite theirs afterwards, which is fine and is why the submission stays open. If you are behind, do half the room and say the other half opens next week. Anything that sounds too big or not really theirs, note it and tell Tim rather than correcting it in front of everyone.
