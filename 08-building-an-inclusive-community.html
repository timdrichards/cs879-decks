Building an
Inclusive Community

Act two: designing for learning

Where we are

Unit 7 was the student in front of you. Today is the room.

Specifically what happens when you see something and say nothing. Almost nobody here would defend exclusion. Nearly everyone has worked out what they should have said about four hours later.

What it actually looks like

  • A student explains something a groupmate said two minutes earlier
  • One person gets interrupted every time she starts
  • A lab group forms and someone ends up alone
  • A joke lands badly and everyone moves on quickly

None of these look reportable. All of them accumulate.

THINK · PAIR · 5 MIN

Name one

Something you have witnessed in a CS setting, as a student or a TA.

You do not have to share it with the room. Just have one in mind.

Interruption is not confrontation

"Hold on, I want to hear the end of what Priya was saying."

  • Eight words
  • Names nobody as a villain
  • Changes the room
The bystander research is consistent: what works is small and immediate, not large and delayed.

You have standing

You are not a peer in that room.

When a TA says the conversation is going to include everyone, it does, because you set the norms whether or not you mean to.

Behavior, not character

Puts them on trial

  • "That was offensive"
  • They defend rather than reconsider

Can be answered

  • "That joke didn't land for me"
  • Leaves room for a different conversation
IN GROUPS · 12 MIN

Say the actual sentence

Take a scenario. Work out who has power, whether you interrupt now or afterward, and the exact words you would use.

Then say them out loud to your group.

Some of it you fix by design

  • Rotate who reports back for the group
  • Call on people by name
  • Ask the question before taking volunteers
  • Structure tasks so participation is required
Unit 4 was partly about this. A well designed task prevents the situation you would otherwise have to interrupt.

It shows up in the artifacts too

Datasets, examples, default assumptions in code.

Pointing that out in a technical context is often easier and better received than pointing it out in a social one.

It is also more clearly part of your job.

The move

Small and immediately,
rather than perfect and later

What works is not the well-crafted intervention you think of that evening.

It is the eight words in the moment, which are almost always enough, because the point is that someone said anything at all.

Where this shows up later

If you stay in academia

  • Lab culture is set by whoever sets it
  • That will be you, sooner than you think

If you go to industry

  • Team culture works the same way
  • Psychological safety is the measured predictor
Someone says something and the room goes quiet. Everyone is waiting to see whether you say anything.

And if neither is yours: what do you want from the job you have?

Pick one structural change to your own section and make it this week.

Rotate the reporter. Call on people by name. Start with the question rather than the volunteer.
