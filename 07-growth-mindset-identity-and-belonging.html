Act two: designing for learning

Where we are

Unit 6 was fair access to help. Today is the student who has access and does not use it.

Because they have decided they are not the kind of person who is good at this. That belief is common in CS, strongest among students already underrepresented, and more responsive to what a TA says than most people expect.

Today

  • Fixed and growth mindsets, and how to hear the difference
  • Imposter phenomenon
  • LARA, for the conversation going badly
  • Recognizing distress, and who to call

The numbers behind this

  • High failure rates in introductory CS courses
  • Graduation rates that differ sharply by group
  • Students who leave saying they were not the right kind of person

None of that is about ability. Much of it is about belonging.

THINK · PAIR · SHARE · 6 MIN

Identify the mindset

  • "I don't have much experience with hardware yet."
  • "I'm bad at math."
  • "I could never debug that as fast as you just did."
  • "I haven't figured out pointers."

Which are fixed, which are growth, and which are genuinely ambiguous?

Praise the process

Costly

  • "You're so smart"
  • Makes the next failure evidence against the compliment

Useful

  • "That was a good way to narrow down the bug"
  • Gives them something repeatable

Imposter phenomenon

Talented students, especially from underrepresented groups, frequently believe they are the only one struggling.

Saying out loud that this is common, and that you have felt it, is not a platitude.

It is information they do not have. Share a story of your own struggle. It works better than encouragement.

Building a CS identity

  • Interest. You have to like it before you are part of it.
  • Confidence. You have to believe you can succeed.
  • Recognition. Someone has to treat you as a person who does this.
The third one a TA can supply directly. Name what a student did well, by name, in front of others.

LARA

  • Listen. Actually, not while composing your reply.
  • Affirm. Find the part that is true.
  • Respond. To what they said, not what you assumed.
  • Add, or Ask for information.
Worth having in your head before you need it. In the moment you will not invent it.
IN PAIRS · 10 MIN

Dignity and respect in action

Read the LARA handout. Decide who listens first. Take a conversation with real emotional weight.

Then swap, and tell each other what landed.

What will you do if a student says this?

  • "I can't take it anymore."
  • "There's no way out."
  • "Nothing matters anymore."

You will have about four seconds and you will not be expecting it.

The protocol

  • Find somewhere quiet
  • Do not promise to keep it secret, because you may not be able to
  • Offer hope. Say you care and that help exists.
  • Connect them to the people whose job this is
  • Do not leave them alone if you are worried right now
You are not expected to handle this yourself. The Maroon Folder has the warning signs and the numbers.

The move

Respond to the belief,
not just the question

When a student says "I'm bad at this," answering the technical question leaves the belief untouched.

And the belief is what determines whether they come back.
